Field guide

Navigating the atlas

Move through the solar system at any pace, from any point of view. Every orbit is computed, not drawn by hand.

Click

Center the map on a world. The Sun, every planet, Pluto and the Moon are valid centers.

Drag

Pan the map. Hold Shift while dragging to tilt and rotate. Arrow keys pan; Shift + arrows tilt.

Scroll

Zoom around the pointer. Pinch with two fingers on a touchscreen.

Space

Pause or resume the simulation. F fits all orbits, A switches Atlas, R resets the view.

1 — 5

Jump between hour, day, week, month and year per second.

Esc

Close menus and dialogs. ? reopens this guide.

About the model

Planetary positions come from JPL's Keplerian elements for the mean ecliptic and equinox of J2000, with Kepler's equation solved for every frame. The Moon uses a truncated lunar theory. Distances in Orbits are true; marker sizes are exaggerated for visibility. Atlas compresses radial distance logarithmically, keeping the orbital angles intact. Always draws each world’s complete orbit around its parent — planets around the Sun, the Moon around Earth — translated into the current center’s frame at full brightness, with no fading. This is an exploratory orrery, not a precision ephemeris.
